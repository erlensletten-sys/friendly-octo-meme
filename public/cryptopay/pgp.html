Verify with a PGP key

A way to prove it's you — with no email, no password, and nothing for us to leak. Takes about two minutes, even if you've never touched PGP.

What is a PGP key, in plain words?

A PGP key is really two keys that belong together:

  • Your public key — safe to share with anyone, like a mailing address. what's this?
  • Your private key — a secret only you hold, like the key to your front door. what's this?

Anyone can lock a message with your public key, but only your private key can unlock it. That's the whole trick here: we lock a one-time secret to your public key; if you can unlock it, you must be the key's owner.

We never see your private key. Not when you make it, not when you use it. If you paste it into the wrong box by mistake, we refuse it.

Find your public key

You need the public half, as text that starts with -----BEGIN PGP PUBLIC KEY BLOCK-----. Pick your tool:

Terminal (GnuPG / gpg) 
gpg --armor --export you@example.com
Prints your public key. Copy everything from BEGIN to END.
Kleopatra (Windows / Gpg4win) Right-click your certificate → Export… → save as a .asc file → open it and copy the text. (Don't use "Export Secret Keys".)
GPG Suite (macOS) Select your key in GPG Keychain → Export → leave "Include secret key" unchecked → open the file and copy the text.
Phone (OpenKeychain on Android) Open the key → menu → Share public key → copy the text.

Paste your public key

It should start with -----BEGIN PGP PUBLIC KEY BLOCK-----. If yours says PRIVATE, stop — that's your secret one. Go back and export the public one.
